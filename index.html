Product

A workflow builder that speaks compliance natively.

No glue code. No rogue cloud calls. Every node is audited, versioned, and signed. Every agent runs where your data lives.

01 /Drag domain nodes

Start from the node library: DICOM readers, FHIR resources, PHI de-identifiers, policy guards. Typed ports reject incompatible connections before runtime.

02 /Wire in local agents

Drop in LLM agents backed by your choice of on-device models. Llama, Qwen, in-house — bound to the pipeline with tool-use policies and rate limits.

03 /Run on your hardware

The DrawLabs runtime executes on-prem — your workstation, your VPC, your air-gapped cluster. Data, prompts, and model weights stay inside the boundary.

04 /Burst to cloud, selectively

One-click lift for the heavy steps — after de-identification, with per-node destination controls. Audit log captures every byte crossing the boundary.

Architecture / moat

Local-first by default.
Cloud-elastic by choice.

The runtime is a single binary. It runs on a laptop, a workstation, or a rack — with the same semantics. Bursting is a policy decision, not an architectural one.

Why now — and why us.

Frontier models got good enough to matter in regulated work. On-device inference got fast enough to run them inside the boundary. Compliance buyers are being asked to ship AI — and told they can't move the data. DrawLabs is the path through.

Read the thesis ↓
  • 01Local runtime, inside the boundary. Once the runtime is deployed, it becomes part of the customer's compliance posture — not a vendor dependency.
  • 02Domain packs built with experts. Typed nodes for DICOM, FHIR, de-identification — shipped as signed packs, not stitched together from generic SDKs.
  • 03A canvas the compliance team can read. Visual pipelines make reviewers and builders successful at the same time.
  • 04Burst to cloud on your terms. Heavy steps can route out after de-identification — fully optional, always audited.
